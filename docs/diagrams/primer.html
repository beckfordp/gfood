gfood: a food-delivery primer and story map

A three-sided marketplace on gluon's services: customers order, restaurants cook, couriers deliver. What follows is the domain in one sitting, the people it serves, and a first cut of the map from walking skeleton to a trustworthy service.

Part 1

The domain in one sitting

Every order is a promise kept by three parties who never meet in advance. The platform's job is coordination under a clock: get hot food from a kitchen it doesn't run, by a courier it doesn't employ, to a customer who is watching the minutes.

Customer

Wants the food they chose, hot, when promised. Judges the service by the ETA and by how problems are fixed.

Restaurant

Wants extra orders without chaos at the pass. Controls the menu, what's sold out, opening hours and prep time.

Courier

Wants paid work with little waiting. Chooses when to be online and which jobs to take.

Platform (gfood)

Matches the three, sets fees and the ETA, takes the payment, pays out, and owns the refund when it goes wrong.

An order's life, against the clock

  1. Placed and paid. Payment is authorised at checkout; the basket belongs to one restaurant only. customer
  2. Restaurant notified. The tablet sounds until someone acknowledges it. restaurant
  3. Accepted, with a prep time. Or rejected (sold out, too busy). No answer within a few minutes cancels the order automatically and refunds it. restaurant
  4. ETA set. Prep time plus courier travel; this is the promise the customer watches. platform
  5. Courier dispatched. Timed so the courier arrives as the food is ready, not before; an offer goes to one courier, who accepts or declines within seconds. courier
  6. Food ready, picked up. Waiting at the restaurant is the courier's biggest loss of earnings. courier
  7. On the way. The customer sees the courier move and the ETA update. customer
  8. Delivered. With proof (a photo or a code); payment captured, payouts accrue. courier
  9. Afterwards. Ratings, and any complaint: missing item, cold food, never arrived. support

Customer can't be reached at the doorContact attempts, a timer, then left or returned; usually no refund
Missing or wrong itemSupport refunds the item; repeat claims flagged
Customer cancelsFree before the restaurant accepts; after that, usually charged

Words the domain uses

Basket
The order being built; always from one restaurant.
Modifiers (options, extras)
Size, toppings, "no onions". They change price and make the menu a tree, not a list.
Sold out ("86'd")
Kitchen slang for an item that has run out, toggled by staff during service.
Prep time
The restaurant's estimate on accepting; drives dispatch timing and the ETA.
Delivery zone
The area a restaurant delivers to; a postcode outside it sees the restaurant as unavailable.
Dispatch
Choosing a courier for an order and when to send them.
Offer
A job shown to one courier with a short accept window; declines pass it on.
Stacking (batching)
One courier carrying two orders on one trip. Efficient, and a source of late food.
Proof of delivery
Photo, handover code or signature, used to settle "never arrived" claims.
Commission
The platform's share of the basket, taken from the restaurant's payout.

Part 4

Open questions for you

  1. Roles in user-service, or separate accounts?Restaurant staff and couriers need sign-in in Releases 1 and 2. Roles keep one identity service; it may be the first platform API change gfood asks for.
  2. Authorise then capture, or charge at checkout?Real platforms differ. Authorise-then-capture makes rejections cheap (release the hold); check what payment-service supports.
  3. Order states: whose are they?If order-service's lifecycle can't express "accepted, ready, picked up" without gfood nouns, those states live in restaurant- and dispatch-service, and order only knows placed, paid, completed, cancelled.
  4. Geography in the sandbox.No map tiles or geocoding are reachable. A seeded grid of postcodes with coordinates and straight-line distance keeps dispatch and ETAs real enough.
  5. Live updates.Tracking wants push (SSE or WebSocket). gshop polls today; is a push channel a platform capability or a gfood one?
  6. One city, one zone for the skeleton?Suggested: yes. Zones per restaurant arrive in Release 1.
