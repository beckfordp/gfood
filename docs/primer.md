# The domain in one sitting

Every order is a promise kept by three parties who never meet in advance. The
platform's job is coordination under a clock: get hot food from a kitchen it doesn't
run, by a courier it doesn't employ, to a customer who is watching the minutes.

## The marketplace

| Side | Wants |
| --- | --- |
| **Customer** | The food they chose, hot, when promised. Judges the service by the ETA and by how problems are fixed. |
| **Restaurant** | Extra orders without chaos at the pass. Controls the menu, what's sold out, opening hours and prep time. |
| **Courier** | Paid work with little waiting. Chooses when to be online and which jobs to take. |
| **Platform (gfood)** | Matches the three, sets fees and the ETA, takes the payment, pays out, and owns the refund when it goes wrong. |

## Where the money goes

An illustrative order. Real platforms' figures vary by market and contract;
commissions in the 25–35% range are commonly published.

| Line | Paid by | Goes to | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Two pizzas and a side | Customer | Restaurant, less commission | £24.00 |
| Delivery fee (distance-based) | Customer | Platform | £2.49 |
| Service fee (a % of the basket, capped) | Customer | Platform | £1.20 |
| Tip | Customer | Courier, in full | £1.50 |
| **Customer pays** | | | **£29.19** |
| Commission at 30% of the basket | Restaurant | Platform | −£7.20 |
| Courier's job fee (distance plus waiting time) | Platform | Courier | £4.10 |

So the restaurant receives £16.80, the courier £5.60, and the platform keeps £6.79
before payment costs. A small-order fee usually tops up baskets under a minimum.

## An order's life, against the clock

1. **Placed and paid** (customer). Payment is authorised at checkout; the basket
   belongs to one restaurant only.
2. **Restaurant notified** (restaurant). The tablet sounds until someone acknowledges it.
3. **Accepted, with a prep time** (restaurant). Or rejected (sold out, too busy). No
   answer within a few minutes cancels the order automatically and refunds it.
4. **ETA set** (platform). Prep time plus courier travel; this is the promise the
   customer watches.
5. **Courier dispatched** (courier). Timed so the courier arrives as the food is ready,
   not before; an offer goes to one courier, who accepts or declines within seconds.
6. **Food ready, picked up** (courier). Waiting at the restaurant is the courier's
   biggest loss of earnings.
7. **On the way** (customer). The customer sees the courier move and the ETA update.
8. **Delivered** (courier). With proof (a photo or a code); payment captured, payouts
   accrue.
9. **Afterwards** (support). Ratings, and any complaint: missing item, cold food, never
   arrived.

## Where it goes wrong

| What happens | Usual handling |
| --- | --- |
| Restaurant rejects or doesn't answer | Cancel, full refund, tell the customer at once |
| An item is sold out after ordering | Restaurant removes it (partial refund) or rejects the order |
| No courier accepts | Re-offer, widen the radius; after a limit, tell restaurant and customer, possibly cancel |
| Courier waits long at the restaurant | Courier paid for waiting; ETA updated |
| Customer can't be reached at the door | Contact attempts, a timer, then left or returned; usually no refund |
| Missing or wrong item | Support refunds the item; repeat claims flagged |
| Customer cancels | Free before the restaurant accepts; after that, usually charged |

## Words the domain uses

- **Basket**: the order being built; always from one restaurant.
- **Modifiers (options, extras)**: size, toppings, "no onions". They change price and
  make the menu a tree, not a list.
- **Sold out ("86'd")**: kitchen slang for an item that has run out, toggled by staff
  during service.
- **Prep time**: the restaurant's estimate on accepting; drives dispatch timing and the
  ETA.
- **Delivery zone**: the area a restaurant delivers to; a postcode outside it sees the
  restaurant as unavailable.
- **Dispatch**: choosing a courier for an order and when to send them.
- **Offer**: a job shown to one courier with a short accept window; declines pass it on.
- **Stacking (batching)**: one courier carrying two orders on one trip. Efficient, and a
  source of late food.
- **Proof of delivery**: photo, handover code or signature, used to settle "never
  arrived" claims.
- **Commission**: the platform's share of the basket, taken from the restaurant's
  payout.
